## 3. Worked maths

### 3.1 Sampling: how much do 10 leaves tell us? (question 2)

Model: each leaf independently affected with probability p (incidence in the sampled rows), X affected of n = 10, X ~ Binomial(10, p). Assumes a perfect classifier and independent leaves; 3.3 and 3.4 relax both.

95% intervals for p:

| x of 10 | Wilson | Clopper-Pearson (exact) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.000 to 0.278 | 0.000 to 0.308 |
| 1 | 0.018 to 0.404 | 0.003 to 0.445 |
| 2 | 0.057 to 0.510 | 0.025 to 0.556 |
| 3 | 0.108 to 0.603 | 0.067 to 0.652 |
| 5 | 0.237 to 0.763 | 0.187 to 0.813 |
| 10 | 0.722 to 1.000 | 0.692 to 1.000 |

One-sided 95% upper bound for 0 of 10: 1 - 0.05^(1/10) = 0.259. So "no rust on 10 leaves" means "probably fewer than about 1 leaf in 4 affected", not "no rust".

Operating characteristic of the current rule (rules.json: 0 = healthy, 1 to 2 = "few", 3 or more = "many"):

| true p | P(0 of 10) | P(1 to 2, "few") | P(3+, "many") |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.599 | 0.390 | 0.012 |
| 0.10 | 0.349 | 0.581 | 0.070 |
| 0.20 | 0.107 | 0.570 | 0.322 |
| 0.30 | 0.028 | 0.355 | 0.617 |
| 0.40 | 0.006 | 0.161 | 0.833 |
| 0.50 | 0.001 | 0.054 | 0.945 |

Formula: P(X >= 3) = 1 - sum_{k=0..2} C(10,k) p^k (1-p)^(10-k). Example p = 0.3: P(0) = 0.0282, P(1) = 0.1211, P(2) = 0.2335, so P(X >= 3) = 1 - 0.3828 = 0.617.

Reading: at 10% incidence the rule says "many" 7% of the time (false alarm); at 30% it says "many" only 62% of the time (miss 38%). The bands are a coarse screen, not a measurement. That is acceptable for a trigger that routes to a person, and it must be said in those words.

With 1 or 2 unsure leaves (allowed by rule 1), n falls: P(X >= 3 | p = 0.3) is 0.537 at n = 9 and 0.448 at n = 8. The rule should use the count of classified leaves as n, or the officer view should show "3 of 8 classified", not "3 of 10".

### 3.2 Sample size to separate the bands (question 2)

Target: tell p0 = 0.10 ("few") from p1 = 0.30 ("many"), one-sided error alpha on p0, power 1 - beta on p1.

Normal approximation: n = [ (z_alpha sqrt(p0 q0) + z_beta sqrt(p1 q1)) / (p1 - p0) ]^2.
alpha = beta = 0.10: z = 1.2816; sqrt(0.09) = 0.300, sqrt(0.21) = 0.458; n = [(0.3845 + 0.5873) / 0.2]^2 = 4.859^2 = 23.6, so 24.

Exact binomial search (smallest n and cut k with P(X >= k | 0.10) <= alpha and P(X >= k | 0.30) >= 1 - beta):

| alpha | beta | n | act if at least | false alarm | power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 0.10 | 25 | 5 | 0.098 | 0.910 |
| 0.05 | 0.20 | 25 | 6 | 0.033 | 0.807 |
| 0.05 | 0.10 | 33 | 7 | 0.042 | 0.906 |
| 0.05 | 0.05 | 41 | 8 | 0.048 | 0.954 |
| current | | 10 | 3 | 0.070 | 0.617 |

Conclusion: 10 leaves cannot separate 10% from 30% with both errors under 10%; 25 leaves can. Do not change the protocol before the freeze (25 photos is a long Saturday for Noor, and Nuru's six-leaf evidence supports a small n for detection). Say it in the docs: "10 leaves is enough to detect rust that is common in the worst rows; it is not enough to measure incidence. An officer survey uses more."

Is "worst-looking rows" biased? Yes, by design: it estimates incidence in the worst rows, which is an upper-biased estimate of plot incidence. For a trigger whose job is "is there a problem worth acting on", sampling the worst rows raises sensitivity, which is the right direction. The summary must say "6 of 10 leaves from the rows you picked", never "60% of your farm".

### 3.3 Clustering: why "one leaf per tree" matters (question 2)

Design effect for m leaves per tree with intra-tree correlation rho: DEFF = 1 + (m - 1) rho, effective n = 10 / DEFF. rho is an assumption (no Kenyan figure found); rust spreads by rain splash within a tree (S03), so leaves on one tree are correlated.

| leaves per tree | rho (assumed) | DEFF | effective n |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | any | 1.00 | 10.0 |
| 2 | 0.3 | 1.30 | 7.7 |
| 5 | 0.3 | 2.20 | 4.5 |
| 5 | 0.5 | 3.00 | 3.3 |

Telling Noor to take one leaf from each of 10 trees costs nothing and keeps the 3.1 numbers honest.

### 3.4 Classifier error propagation and Rogan-Gladen (question 4)

Observed per-leaf positive rate: q = p Se + (1 - p)(1 - Sp).

In-domain rust (metrics.json): recall Se = 0.993, precision 0.989, support 1,221 of 6,714. TP = 0.9934 x 1221 = 1213.0; FP = TP (1/precision - 1) = 13.0; negatives = 5,493; Sp = 1 - 13.0/5493 = 0.9976. In-domain the correction is negligible (q at p = 0.1 is 0.101).

Under shift we have no rust Se or Sp. Two labelled assumptions show the shape:

| scenario | p = 0 | p = 0.1 | p = 0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| in-domain (0.993, 0.998) | q 0.002, P(many) 0.000 | q 0.101, P(many) 0.073 | q 0.300, P(many) 0.616 |
| shift A, assumed (0.80, 0.95) | q 0.050, P(many) 0.012 | q 0.125, P(many) 0.120 | q 0.275, P(many) 0.548 |
| shift B, assumed (0.60, 0.85) | q 0.150, P(many) 0.180 | q 0.195, P(many) 0.307 | q 0.285, P(many) 0.576 |

Under shift B a rust-free plot gets the "many leaves show rust, spray" card 18% of the time. That is the gate-relevant number we need to measure (F1), not guess.

Rogan-Gladen: p_hat = (q_hat + Sp - 1) / (Se + Sp - 1). With shift A and x = 3 of 10: (0.30 + 0.95 - 1) / (0.75) = 0.333; the exact interval maps to 0.022 to 0.803. With n = 10 the correction moves the point estimate a little and the interval is already wide, and it needs Se and Sp in the target domain, which we do not have. Recommendation: do not correct in the app. Show raw counts plus the unsure count. Put this sensitivity table in EVALUATION as "what classifier error does to a 10-leaf count".

The `mixed_problems` rule (F6). With 3 rust leaves and 7 others, and a per-leaf false "other problem" rate e:

| e | P(at least 1 false other in 7) | P(at least 2 in 7) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.01 | 0.068 | 0.002 |
| 0.05 | 0.302 | 0.044 |
| 0.10 | 0.522 | 0.150 |

In-domain phoma recall is 0.924; a plot with 3 phoma leaves has at least one leaf called something else with probability 1 - 0.924^3 = 0.211, which under the current rule turns a phoma plot into "mixed problems". Both outcomes route to the officer, so this is noise, not harm; for rust it suppresses the timely card.

Abstention at plot level. In-domain unsure rate u = 1 - coverage = 0.066 (test). P(3 or more unsure of 10) at u = 0.06 is 0.019. Under shift: u = 0.2 gives 0.322, u = 0.3 gives 0.617, u = 0.5 gives 0.945. So if shifted photos are mostly low-confidence, rule 1 (`too_many_unsure`) already does the fail-safe at plot level. If they are high-confidence wrong, it does not. F1 decides which world we are in.

### 3.5 Calibration and abstention: max-softmax vs conformal (question 5)

What we have: temperature T = 3.5 by NLL grid search on in-domain val, then the lowest max-softmax threshold keeping 95% accuracy on accepted val leaves: thr = 0.583, val coverage 0.940 at 0.952 accuracy, test coverage 0.934 at 0.975 (metrics.json). The `calibrate()` k-loop bug is fixed (train.py lines 222 to 231 keep the largest k).

Split conformal (LAC score s = 1 - p_true): with n = 6,764 val leaves and alpha = 0.05, take the ceil((n + 1)(1 - alpha)) = 6,427th smallest score, quantile level 6427/6764 = 0.9502. The prediction set C(x) = {y : p_y >= 1 - q_hat} then contains the true label with probability at least 0.95, marginally, if the new leaf is exchangeable with val. Mondrian (class-conditional) conformal gives the same guarantee per class, for example rust recall of at least 95%.

What changes in practice: a singleton set is close to "max-softmax above a threshold", so behaviour on accepted leaves would barely change. Conformal adds a clean sentence ("the true label is in the set 95% of the time on Kenyan crops") and a natural "rust or miner, not sure" output.

What does not change: both guarantees need exchangeability. F4 shows that even our own val and test are not exchangeable (z = 10.7), and Uganda is far from it. Under covariate shift, weighted conformal (Tibshirani et al., NeurIPS 2019) needs the likelihood ratio between deployment and training inputs, which we cannot estimate without deployment photos.

Decision: keep max-softmax. Do the decision-group version (F12) because it removes phoma vs miner confusion from the accept decision and raises coverage at equal decision accuracy (the max of grouped probabilities is never below the max class probability). Conformal goes to "after submission". In docs, never call the threshold "calibrated for the field".

### 3.7 Decision rule under uncertainty (questions 1 and 3)

Expected-loss form: two actions, spray programme (cost C, which includes money, labour and the harm S03 notes) or not (loss L if incidence is high, defined as p > p*). Spray if P(p > p* | data) > C / L.

Posterior with a flat Beta(1,1) prior after x of 10: Beta(1 + x, 11 - x).

| x of 10 | posterior mean | P(p > 0.10) | P(p > 0.20) | P(p > 0.30) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.083 | 0.314 | 0.086 | 0.020 |
| 1 | 0.167 | 0.697 | 0.322 | 0.113 |
| 2 | 0.250 | 0.910 | 0.617 | 0.313 |
| 3 | 0.333 | 0.981 | 0.839 | 0.570 |
| 4 | 0.417 | 0.997 | 0.950 | 0.790 |

Smallest x that justifies spraying, by C / L (flat prior):

| p* | C/L 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| 0.20 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| 0.30 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

Reading:
- "Act at 3 of 10" is equivalent to C / L of about 0.6 to 0.7 at p* = 0.2: it assumes spraying is costly relative to the loss it prevents.
- If spraying is cheap relative to the loss (C / L at or below 0.3, assumption), the flat prior says spray even at 0 of 10 when p* = 0.1. That is the logic of the Kenyan calendar programme in S03 (spray mid-October before the short rains, repeat after three weeks) for susceptible varieties (SL28, SL34, still common in central Kenya, S14).
- So the leaf check changes the decision in three cases: the farmer treats spraying as costly (S03: chemicals "expensive and injurious", "last resort"), the problem is not rust (leaf miner: spraying makes it worse, S17), or the officer has to choose which farms to visit.

Recommendation: keep decision 2 (act / wait / ask). Do not switch to a Bayesian rule in the app: the prior and C / L are not known for Kenya, and an officer can audit a count. Do three cheap things: (a) state in the docs which C / L the "3 of 10" band implies, so an officer can move the cut-off knowingly; (b) fix the pre-rains wording (F7); (c) frame the pitch as "is rust already in my worst rows before the rains, or is it something where spraying is wrong", not "should I spray".

A decision analyst's best switch would be "spend on the pre-rains copper programme this season: yes, no, or ask", with the calendar as the default and the leaf check as information that can overturn it. It is the same flow with different card wording, so it is not worth reopening decision 2 at this hour.

### 3.8 Evaluation validity (question 6)

- Leakage: pHash found 1,244,500 near-duplicate pairs and 24,236 clusters in 63,687 files (STATUS M1). A single clique with that many pairs would hold about 1,578 images (n(n-1)/2 = 1,244,500), so a few very large clusters are likely. Splitting by cluster stops copies crossing splits, but one large easy cluster landing in test inflates test accuracy. That is the most likely cause of the 4-point val/test gap (z = 10.7: SE = sqrt(0.930 x 0.070/6764 + 0.970 x 0.030/6714) = 0.0037; 0.0401/0.0037 = 10.7).
- The pooled 0.970 includes not_leaf (F1 0.989), which is easy. Disease-only accuracy should be reported.
- Uganda is augmented, healthy-only and 256 px: it tests the healthy class only and cannot measure rust recall. The full download (rust `1200_*`, phoma `2300_*`) is needed for a rust number.
- RoCoLe: not on disk, so no field rust number exists at all. Say so rather than leave a 0.
- The 254 wild photos are hint-labelled; 58 are research-grade iNaturalist rust. They give a rust detection rate on messy photos with n = 58: at 50% observed, the Wilson interval is about 0.37 to 0.63. Report it as "messy photos, labels not checked by an agronomist".

## 5. Two hard questions a technical judge will ask

**Q1. "Your model gets 2% on the only real phone photos you tested. Why should Noor trust a card that says 'many leaves show rust, spray'?"**

Best honest answer: "She should not trust the model alone, and the design does not ask her to. On Kenyan lesion crops it is 93 to 97%; on 424 Ugandan phone photos it recognised 7 healthy leaves, and on those photos it said 'not sure' for [X]% [PENDING F1]. Three things stand between that error and a spray: the photo protocol (one leaf per tree, underside, plain sheet), plot-level rules (3 or more unsure leaves always goes to a person; two problems go to the officer), and the card itself, which sends product and dose to the cooperative. We publish the bad number because field accuracy on Noor's leaves is unknown, and the officer-correction loop is how it gets measured." If F1 shows confident false rust, add: "so we added a plot-level gate that abstains when the photos do not look like anything we trained on; it was set on validation data before we ran it on Uganda."

**Q2. "Kenya's rust programme is calendar-based. What does a 10-leaf count change, and is 10 leaves enough?"**

Best honest answer: "Ten leaves detect rust that is common in the rows Noor is worried about; they do not measure incidence. At 30% incidence the rule says 'many' 62% of the time, at 10% it wrongly says 'many' 7% of the time, and 10 clean leaves only rule out more than about 1 in 4. Separating 10% from 30% with both errors under 10% needs about 25 leaves. The count matters in three cases: when spraying is costly for her (our 3-of-10 band corresponds to a cost-to-loss ratio of about 0.6), when the problem is leaf miner, where spraying makes it worse, and when the officer has to choose which of 1,380 farmers to visit. Every cut-off is labelled 'assumption, officer to confirm' because we found no Kenyan threshold."
