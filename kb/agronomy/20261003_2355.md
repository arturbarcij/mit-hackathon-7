## 3. Corrected sampling protocol (as Noor would follow it)

Purpose: get a fair count of how many mature leaves carry a problem, without spending more than 20 minutes on a picking Saturday.

1. **When.** Saturday mid-morning, after the dew has dried off the leaves. Not in rain. If she is picking cherry that day, do it while picking (step 3 can be "one leaf from every tree where you start a new row").
2. **What to carry.** An old exercise book. The phone stays at the house.
3. **Which trees.** Walk through the coffee in a W: in at one corner, across, back, across again, so you pass through the good rows and the bad rows. Stop at 10 trees spread along that walk. Not 10 trees in one row. Skip trees sprayed in the last two weeks if you can. (W walk and middle third: K6. One leaf per tree for spread across rows: practice, unsourced. K6 found more leaves per tree at least as efficient as more trees, so 5 trees with 2 leaves each is acceptable if time is short.)
4. **Which branch.** At each tree, take a side branch from the middle of the tree height, about chest height on a normal tree, not the bottom skirt and not the top. (K6 middle third; bottom leaves overstate rust: practice, unsourced.)
5. **Which leaf.** Count leaf pairs from the tip of that branch. Take one leaf from the 3rd or 4th pair: a full-size, dark green, mature leaf. Not the soft new leaves at the tip, not an old yellow leaf about to fall. (4th pair, mid canopy: K2. Rust shows on mature leaves: practice, unsourced.)
6. **Take it whatever it looks like.** Do not choose a sick-looking leaf over a clean one. The count only means something if the leaf is chosen by its position. If you see something odd elsewhere (black berries, a dying branch, insects), remember it for the officer; do not swap it in.
7. **Carry it flat.** Put each leaf flat between the pages of the exercise book, underside up. Do not crush it into a pocket or a plastic bag in the sun: it sweats, curls and the orange powder rubs off. Do not wipe the underside. (practice, unsourced)
8. **Photograph within an hour.** At the house, in bright shade outside, not direct sun. Lay each leaf on a plain page, underside up, one leaf per photo, the leaf filling the screen. The daughter can hold the phone. A coffee leaf keeps its colour and spots for a few hours in shade; after half a day in the sun it curls and yellows (practice, unsourced).
9. **Check for powder yourself.** Before you accept a rust answer: rub the underside of a leaf the app called rust. Yellow-orange powder on your finger is rust. No powder: press "Ask the officer". (S03, S17 for the powder; rub test practice, unsourced.)
10. **Afterwards.** Wash hands. Burn or bury the 10 leaves, or drop them where you stand in the coffee, not near the house or other farms. (practice, unsourced)

What changes in the app text: only `how_to_pick_leaves` (steps 3 to 7 in two short sentences plus a picture), `how_to_photograph` (step 8) and a rub check line in the rust cards (step 9). Suggested `how_to_pick_leaves` en: "Walk across all your coffee and stop at 10 trees. From the middle of each tree, take the third or fourth leaf from the tip of a branch, whatever it looks like. Carry them flat in a book, underside up." Swahili for content-voice and L3 to write.

## 5. What the tool must say it cannot see (plain words, for a card or the "not sure" line)

"This check looks at 10 coffee leaves only. It cannot see:
- your berries: black, rotting or falling green berries (berry disease), or small holes at the tip (berry borer);
- insects on the tree, such as the bugs that spoil beans (antestia);
- your soil: if it is too sour or short of fertiliser;
- old trees, trees that bore very heavily last year, or branches dying back;
- whether the rain failed when the trees were flowering;
- which kind of coffee you grow;
- how much cherry you picked, sold or lost.
For any of these, ask the officer or the factory."

Short spoken version for every result: "We only looked at 10 leaves. Leaves are one reason for a small harvest. Berries, soil, old trees and rain are not checked."

## 6. Diagnosis: why central Kenya coffee yields drop (Q1)

Order is my judgement for a smallholding at about 1,700 to 1,900 m in Nyeri or Kirinyaga, in a season where the farmer "cannot say why".

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Coffee berry disease on SL28/SL34 in wet spells | No (berries) | Green berries turn black and drop | S17 ("crop losses can be more than 80%"), S14 |
| 3 | Biennial bearing and overbearing dieback after a heavy year | No | Last year was big; branch tips died back | K1, K2 |
| 4 | Old, unpruned or never-stumped trees | No | Tall, bare lower stems, few new bearing branches | K2 (stumping), K5 |
| 5 | Leaf rust defoliation, mostly hitting next season | Yes, the only one | Bare branches after the rains; orange powder | S03 |
| 6 | Rain failure or erratic rain at flowering and berry expansion | No | Poor flowering, light berries | K4 ("crystalizing drought"), practice |
| 7 | Antestia, berry borer | No | Bugs on berries; "zebra" beans at drying; holes in berries | K1 |
| 8 | Soil acidity and poor nutrition | Partly (brown eye spot, yellowing) | Ferns and "poverty grass" between the coffee | K2 ("presence of fern and poverty grass"), K1 (pH 4.4 to 5.4) |

Does a leaf-only check address the right problem? Partly. It addresses the one cause Noor can still act on this month (rust, before the short rains, which protects next season's crop). It does not explain this season's drop for most farms. So the honest frame for the demo is: "the map tells the cooperative where to look, the leaf check rules rust in or out and times the spray, everything else goes to the officer with a better referral."

Proposed `other_causes` card text (en, for content-voice): "A smaller harvest has many causes. Tell the officer if: green berries turned black or fell; the trees bore very heavily last year and branch tips died; the trees are old and not pruned for years; you could not buy fertiliser this year; ferns or poverty grass grow between the coffee; the rains failed when the trees flowered." ## 7. Timing in October (Q3)

- **Right:** S03 puts the first rust spray in mid-October just before the short rains and a second three weeks later. KMSA's OND 2026 forecast puts onset in the 2nd to 3rd week of October for the coffee counties (EVIDENCE E10). `pre_short_rains` (1 Oct to 5 Nov) is a reasonable window. Copper before rain also protects young berries against CBD, so the cooperative will usually run one programme for both (K2; practice).
- **Wrong:** October is main harvest East of the Rift (K4). Everything that assumes a free farm in October is off: "prune now" (F2), "check again next weekend" every weekend, spraying without asking about the wait before picking (F6).
- **Season.json label:** the 1 Jun to 30 Sep window is called "dry". In Nyeri, June to August is the cool, overcast season with drizzle (practice, unsourced), which favours CBD and Phoma. The label does not change advice, but `rust_high_dry` must not say "prune" in that window (F2).
- **Rain wash-off:** see F11.
- **Repeat interval:** "three weeks" for copper is sourced (S03, S17 "21 days"). Infonet gives two weeks during the rains for CBD (K1). Keep "ask the cooperative how often" in the card; the three-week figure is fine as background but should not sound like an order.

- Cooperatives already send bulk SMS for payments and delivery receipts, and hold member numbers (practice, unsourced; EVIDENCE E9 on cooperatives). Routing a referral SMS to a cooperative number is realistic. A cooperative replying with advice by SMS every week is less realistic; the cooperative's field staff or factory manager would batch replies, and the officer would plan a group visit per factory area.
- What the officer needs to decide on a visit: member number, factory, rough number of trees, variety, counts by problem, whether berries are black or falling, when and what class of product was last sprayed, and whether neighbours report the same. Today's JANI1 covers the first and the counts only (F24).
- Will the officer use it? Yes if referrals are few and specific. If every single cercospora or phoma leaf creates a referral, and the model over-calls disease on whole leaves (F1), the queue fills with noise and is ignored within a month (practice, unsourced). Suggest the dashboard sorts single-leaf non-rust referrals as low urgency.

## 11. Trust (Q7)

Builds trust: it speaks her language, it is honest that it looked at 10 leaves, it agrees with what she sees (orange powder), it sends her to the cooperative she already deals with, it gets an answer back.
Breaks trust after one bad answer: telling her to spray when she sees no powder; saying "no problem" while her berries are going black; telling her to prune with cherry on the branches; Swahili that sounds Tanzanian or machine-made; a referral that disappears.

## 12. A higher-value decision? (Q8)

For a farmer in October the money is in the harvest: picking only red ripe cherry and delivering it the same day ("Deliver berries for processing the same day they are picked", K1), and knowing what parchment or cherry is worth before the middleman names a price (PDF p. 16). Decision 5 put price out of scope; I do not ask to reopen it. For the pitch, say plainly that Jani's decision protects next season's crop and that harvest quality and price are the next modules. The cheapest high-value addition before 09:30 is the `other_causes` card, which needs no AI.
